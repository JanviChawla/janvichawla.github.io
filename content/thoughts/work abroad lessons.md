Deloitte offers a US-India immersion program for Business Technology Analysts. Two months into my full-time career, I was accepted to go to Hyderabad and Bangalore to work alongside the USI counterparts on my large healthcare project. Not only was this trip a soul-searching experience but it was also an enlightening one in terms of life and financial lessons, thanks to the four other analysts with me in these cities. Here’s a listicle:

- Charge 9 hours every day

You don’t have to mark 7 hours here and 10 hours there and 11 hours including travel, blah blah blah. No one cares. Just do it. It 100% evens out between working more Monday through Thursday and lighter on Fridays. Nickel and dime-ing your hours is not going to make a difference in the long run, for year-end evaluations or otherwise.

The only time to ignore this advice is if you are GROSSLY overworked or underworked for an extended or seemingly indefinite period of time, in which case you should talk to your leadership to figure out how to reduce your workload or take on more stretch opportunities.

- Spend money

Over the course of our timeline, money will come back. We make less when we’re younger and more when we’re older (ideally). If we keep spending the same, it’ll all come back. Don’t deprive yourself of the joys along the way.

- Save money

To spend money guilt-free, automate the saving part. Once money has been automatically allocated to loan/mortgage/car/rent/etc. payments, 401K and other savings are maxed out, whatever’s left over is for you to enjoy to the fullest. Unfortunately since we do make less money when we’re younger, this means living more smartly and going without the nicest things. We can have nice things, just not the nicest things. It’s okay to show you’re in the top 15% instead of the top 1%. Don’t buy as if you’re in the top 1% when you’re not.
